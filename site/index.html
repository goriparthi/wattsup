See watts up with your Mac.

Live watts, volts and amps in your menu bar, and a charger check that tells you whether the brick is the bottleneck.

Download for Mac View on GitHub

macOS 14 or later. Apple silicon MacBooks. MIT licensed.

Pick your numbers.

Choose from 14 readings: power in or out, voltage, current, Mac uses, battery power, from the wall, charger loss, charger load, battery percent, time, temperature, health, energy mode and powering accessories. Reorder them in Settings, show one line or two stacked rows, and keep them in an outlined capsule, colored by meaning.

The default readout is From the wall, Charger load, Mac uses and Battery percent. Hover any number for a tooltip that names it. Click for a live flow from charger to Mac to battery, with watts on every pipe.

Charger check

Is your charger holding you back?

Hotel desk, airport gate, a borrowed brick. Wattsup compares what your Mac is asking for, system load plus what the battery wants to charge, against what the charger can deliver.

  • Flags a brick pegged at its limit.
  • Flags a battery that drains while plugged in.
  • Suggests a wattage that would remove the bottleneck.
  • Remembers every charger you have used, with peak load and average efficiency for each.

Charger efficiency

Watts delivered divided by watts from the wall, graded Excellent at 94% and up, Good at 90%, Fair at 85%, Poor below that. Under 10 W it is not rated. Wattsup remembers the average efficiency of each charger in Chargers you have used.

Best profile offeredCable check
20V 3A, 60 W

The best profile stops at 20 V and 3 A. Your cable is likely the limit. A 5 A cable rated 100 W or 240 W unlocks more.

Cable check

When a charger's best profile stops at exactly 20 V and 3 A (60 W), Wattsup flags that the cable is likely the limit. Handy when you travel and pack one cable for everything.

Privacy

It only listens to your Mac.

Wattsup reads Apple's own battery and power telemetry on your machine. Your readings never leave it, and the source is right there to check.

No data outReadings never leave your Mac. The only request is a daily update check, and you can turn it off.
No accountNothing to sign up for.
Admin on requestThe only password prompt is changing Energy Mode, because macOS requires it.
No trackingNo analytics of any kind.
Local logsLogs stay on your Mac in ~/Library/Logs/Wattsup. They are only for you to share if something breaks.

Install

Two minutes, start to finish.

  1. Download the DMG from the releases page. The first release is 0.0.1.
  2. Drag Wattsup into Applications and open it.
  3. Pick your menu bar numbers.
